The Breakdown

Why the pet services roll-up works.

The U.S. pet industry is a $158B market with 30+ years of uninterrupted growth. It grew 5.1% through the 2008 recession and 16.2% through 2020, while overall retail contracted in both periods. Yale's Budget Lab classifies non-vet pet services as acyclical, ranked alongside eggs for resistance to business cycles. Vet care is procyclical; non-vet services are not.

Within the market, Pet Services is the target segment — $14.3B, growing at 8.78% CAGR (daycare), and structurally fragmented across 20,000+ independent operators. Private equity poured $51.6B into veterinary consolidation and left non-vet services virtually untouched. No dominant national player exists.

◉ Where to buy

Dog daycare & boarding anchors the platform — recurring membership revenue, RTO-driven demand, ~74% of revenue at Dogtopia. Daycare is work-driven (employment-sensitive), not travel-driven (discretionary).

Grooming is the sticky bolt-on — non-discretionary, recurring, customers don't switch. Training is the high-margin cross-sell with a COVID-puppy behavior backlog. Target 60/40 daycare-grooming to boarding revenue mix as a minimum screen.

◆ The arbitrage

Small single-location businesses trade at 2.77–3.32x SDE. BizBuySell's '25 daycare median is 4.40x SDE. Platform-scale pet services exit to PE at 5–8x EBITDA — the same playbook PE ran in vet care, but with less regulatory complexity and lower entry valuations.

Two cleanest geographic paths: DFW northern suburbs (Frisco, McKinney, Allen) or Charlotte-Raleigh corridor. A 3–5 location cluster in a 30-mile radius provides density for shared staffing, marketing, and brand.

Counter-argument worth pricing in. 22% of pet owners reported reduced spending in '25 (up 10% from '24), pressure is building before a formal recession. Services are normalizing post-COVID and some markets are oversupplied. Mitigation: weight toward grooming (non-discretionary) and daycare (employment-driven), avoid boarding-heavy operations. Time-sensitive — PE is catching on (Mosaic at 72 locations, Frontenac adding 15/year).

The Breakdown

Why European HVAC maintenance rolls up.

European HVAC services is a $15.7B market growing to $23.6B by '31 (~7% CAGR). 94% of operators are micro-businesses; ~52% of HVAC sale attempts collapse before close. The market sits where U.S. HVAC was 5–7 years ago — fragmented, founder-led, and under-consolidated. PE activity and fragmentation metrics confirm an early-stage roll-up window, but the European market may not resolve at U.S. pace because it spans 27 jurisdictions instead of one.

Demand splits into two sides with different durability. Maintenance is anchored by EPBD inspections (legally binding from May 2024) and F-Gas refrigerant phase-down — non-discretionary, recurring, and decoupled from new-build cycles. Install and retrofit is heavily subsidy-driven; heat pump sales fell 22% in '24 vs projections after EHPA flagged tightening schemes across France, Germany, Italy, Wallonia, and Poland. The thesis only works on the maintenance side.

◉ Where to enter

Germany first. Largest market, binding legislation, motivated sellers, attractive entry multiples (~8/10). UK as strong alternative — single jurisdiction, active M&A ecosystem, highest policy score (9/10). Netherlands second once depth is established. France attractive on multiples but subsidy volatility makes demand unpredictable. Italy, Spain, and Eastern Europe are too early.

◆ The pathway

Buy small founder-owned at 3–6x EBITDA, formalise recurring contracts (+2.5x), scale via tuck-ins (+4.0x), add mission-critical end-market exposure (+1.5x), build digital/IoT capability (+1.5x), resolve key-person risk (−1.5x if unresolved). Platform exit to PE or strategic at 10–12x EBITDA. Europe trades at ~1.5x discount to U.S. comparables at the top end.

Five risks that decide outcomes. (1) Labour: 80,000 unfilled certified technician roles across the EU — model headcount before revenue. (2) Owner dependence: 52% deal fail rate — map every customer and supplier before exclusivity. (3) Cross-border complexity: build depth in one country before expanding. (4) Quality scarcity: expect to screen 15–25 targets per one that clears the threshold; low multiples often reflect low quality. (5) Policy-dependent demand: prioritise revenue from mandatory inspection over subsidy-availability work. Non-negotiable screen: >50% recurring revenue.
